# Contactcenter-Management-App (CMA)

The Contactcenter-Management-App (CMA) is Pulse4all's own application for running its contact center on top of a **CRM plus a telephony tool** (today HubSpot and Aircall). Those tools hold the customer and the call, but they don't manage the people doing the work or make them fast: who is clocked in, who covers which skill, who gets the next lead, what the agent should do next, how productive the team is. The CMA is that missing layer for Pulse4all: the workforce, productivity and management layer on top of CRM plus phone. Agents log in to start their day and work from one directive screen that serves them the next task; managers plan and steer their team; leads are routed to the right available agent within seconds.

**For whom:** Pulse4all's own business lines. Built first for Pulse4all Subscriptions (AED subscriptions, HubSpot + Aircall), then configured for Pulse4all Invest, and for Pulse4all US if and when that business line starts. We develop the CMA as standalone technology: its own code, database and screens, connected to the CRM and telephony through adapters rather than built inside HubSpot (see Why standalone). Agents and managers use it at **workspace.pulse4all.app**.

**Setup in one line:** front = the Workspace; backend = PostgreSQL on Cloud SQL, the CRM, Aircall, Make and n8n, all on Cloud Run in the EU.

**Where this goes:** one point where everything about the operation comes together in the Workspace: the agent's next task, the team's day, and reporting for every management layer built from all sources (CRM, telephony, Shopify, NetSuite, email, SMS, chat, the Customer App, the Asset Management Database) joined to the staff data only the CMA has. The version in which all sources are connected is the convergence version; its number is set when the roadmap beyond step 10 is cut (see Roadmap, Convergence). The asset register's devices came in ahead of it, as a read-only mirror with Reports → Maintenance (addition 0008zi, Decision log, 27 October 2026), read from the register's API on a schedule since addition 0008zj.

**Team:** Martin, Joshua, Finn
**Key stakeholders:** Arno (call center manager), Kira, Peter, Bas (dashboards and reporting), Yordi (compliance and data protection)

## How we work

- **Open to the team.** This README and the files in its Documentation map are the shared record of scope, decisions and status, kept in the repository next to the code (`Pulse4all-com/cma`). Dev and prod in Google Cloud are visible to everyone in the build group `cma@pulse4all.com`, and every change in prod is traceable in the audit log and in `records/`.
- **Decisions during the V1 build.** To get a first working version in place without long debates up front, Martin sets the direction and takes the build decisions during V1. Each one is written in the Decision log with its reason, so anyone can see what was decided and why, and anyone in the team can question or reopen it. Decisions that touch someone's own area are confirmed with them: KPI targets, result codes and cohorts with Kira, the role ladder and rosters with Arno, Invest with Peter, data protection and labour rules with Yordi, the final name with Mark (see Open decisions).
- **Google-native audits.** The Gem "CMA Google Cloud Auditor" in Pulse4all's Gemini audits the Google Cloud and Google Workspace setup (IAM, Cloud SQL, Cloud Run, IAP, Cloud Build, secrets, logging, BigQuery, cost) as a second opinion. It advises and never changes anything; its knowledge is this README, re-uploaded after every merge that changes it. Its answers go into the Google Doc "CMA - Gemini audit findings" (shared within pulse4all.com). Each finding is checked against this README and becomes a pull request, a console step, or a Decision log entry with the reason it was rejected. Live audits in the Console (Gemini Cloud Assist) run from an account with viewer roles only, because Cloud Assist acts with the asking user's permissions.

## What we are building, in plain words

**In one sentence:** "We're building our own operating system for the Pulse4all contact center: it hands every agent their next call, keeps the clock, and shows management who's productive and which customers stay." Shorter still: air traffic control for our call center. Technically it is a workforce, productivity and management layer on top of CRM plus telephony, built as standalone technology. Contactcenter-Management-App is a working title; the final name is chosen with Mark.

**Three surfaces of the Workspace (Martin, 17 October 2026; the Desk named 18 October 2026).** Who looks, who acts and who does the work get three surfaces of the Workspace, the Dashboard, the Cockpit and the Desk, each derived from the viewer's permissions, never from a role key (Users and roles, Role and surface); the Workspace stays the name of the whole application (workspace.pulse4all.app). The **Dashboard** is for analytics (Kira, Peter, Bas): monitoring and steering on data and reports, read permissions only (`reports.view`, `performance.team`, the aggregates across business lines through `cma_read` and BigQuery); look. The **Cockpit** is for the call center manager and the supervisor: the same signals, and next to each signal the handle to act on it from the same seat: a coverage gap → add or move a shift, message the team; a lead past the speed-to-lead target → assign an owner, push an alert; a missed call waiting → hand it to an agent; an idle agent → push a message, later serve the next task; a forgotten clock-out → correct the day, add the day, send a notice; a ticket at 75 % of its response time (`tickets.sla_warning_pct`) → reassign; a skill gap → edit skills; look and act. Every handle uses a write path the CMA has or plans (`roster_set_entry`, `send_message`, `correct_workday`, `set_person_skills`, the owner write-back on 0009's outbox, 0011's queue engine), audited and gated by its permission; pro-active steering of the workforce is the point. The **Desk** is the agent's: the directive screen (Feature 8, the agent desk; Roadmap steps 7 and 8): the next task served, the outcome in one press, Aircall embedded, and in time dialing from the screen as the AgentUI trial and Steam Connect did it; do. The ceiling on dialing is Aircall's: one-click dial is plausible (`POST /v1/users/:id/calls`, to verify on the live account, Open decisions); a power or predictive dialer is nothing Aircall exposes, so the queue engine gives a progressive dialer: serve, dial, book, next. Always standalone, bespoke for Pulse4all and copyable: built for Pulse4all's own business lines and run from configuration, so another company on a CRM plus Aircall could run a copy from configuration alone (Guiding principles 1 and 6; the cockpit's signals, thresholds and handles are tenant configuration too), while selling or licensing stays outside this project (Out of scope). Speed and UX are part of the concept, not a finish: a cockpit nobody waits for and a desk that answers every click at once; the targets proposed by the IT audit of 17 October 2026 (server time to first byte p75 ≤ 300 ms and p95 ≤ 800 ms on the agent pages and the Live board, p75 ≤ 600 ms and p95 ≤ 1.5 s on reports; the shell paints within 200 ms; click feedback within 100 ms; to confirm with Martin) are how that is kept true.

**Why we believe it works** (assessment of 6 October 2026, to be revisited with the business case below):

- **Technically doable.** Nothing in it is exotic: Postgres with row-level security, Cloud Run, Next.js, webhooks, a work queue on `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, an outbox. The AgentUI trial proved the hardest algorithmic piece and closed its concurrency gaps; the demo proved the screens. The difficulty is elsewhere: the two-way sync with HubSpot (rate limits, idempotency, conflicts), upstream data quality (the Data first list is longer than the code), the long tail of sources in the convergence version, and the cost of keeping everything configurable while building for Subscriptions first. With three people and AI-assisted development the control layer is weeks, the desk is months, convergence a year or more. The risk is scope, not feasibility.
- **Value and ROI.** Positive internally if two things hold, both measured from day one (see Business case): the desk gets adopted and lifts talk time per paid hour, and speed to lead plus kept callbacks turn into conversions that leak today. The management layer alone has softer returns: correct hours per employer, fewer disputes, compliance, steering for Kira and Peter. Cloud cost is modest; the team's time is the real cost.
- **AI agents will take a growing share of the calls; the need stands.** Hand-entered dispositions and scripted screens will fade as AI fills forms and infers outcomes from transcripts. What stays is the layer underneath: the system of record for work (who did what, when, for which customer, with what outcome), the queue that serves tasks, the reporting that joins outcomes to time and cost, and the compliance trail that AFM, FCA and GDPR demand from humans and machines alike. A work queue does not care whether the worker is a person or an AI agent; `app_user` gets a worker kind when the first AI agent works a queue, and attribution and reporting carry over unchanged. Build API-first (Open principle) so AI tools consume the CMA rather than replace it.
- **Why build rather than buy or stretch HubSpot.** HubSpot and Aircall focus on the CRM and the telephony, not on running the team, and the join of channel events with clock, roster and employer is outside both (see Why standalone). Workforce-management tools that integrate with Aircall do exist; a comparison with them is in Open decisions, so the choice to build stays checked. What makes the CMA valuable to Pulse4all is the operational knowledge and data it captures, not the code itself.

## Guiding principles

1. **Configure, don't hardcode.** Nothing specific to a business line, to HubSpot or to Aircall is hardcoded. Markets, teams, skills, channels, statuses, campaigns, result codes, rules and thresholds are tenant configuration; the CRM and the telephony tool are adapters per tenant; system names appear only as data values. We build for Subscriptions first and add another adapter *type* only when a business line moves to another CRM or telephony tool, not before. Being CRM-agnostic is not a future option but a V1 fact: Pulse4all itself runs a separate HubSpot portal and Aircall account per business line (Subs, Invest, later US), so every adapter is configured per tenant with its own credentials, ids and mappings from the first increment.
2. **Open.** The data layer is accessible to other applications through an API, so others can read from it, write to it and build on top of it.
3. **Right the first time.** Multi-tenancy (data strictly separated per business line), roles and permissions, and a change history are part of the foundation from the first table, not added later.
4. **Step by step, straight to production.** Build one working piece in dev, verify it, promote it to production, then start the next piece in dev. Production runs from the first increment; we never build the whole platform first. Always take the clean choice over the quick shortcut.
5. **Use the right tool for each job.** Don't force one system to do everything.
6. **Configurable by default (development rule, 6 October 2026).** Every feature is built so a Pulse4all business line with its own CRM portal and telephony account can start from configuration alone, without code changes. Business-line specifics are tenant configuration and seed data, never code; anything vendor-specific sits behind an adapter interface.
7. **Postgres is the operational core.** Customer facts are owned by the CRM; the work itself (tasks, time, rosters, messages, outcomes, productivity) lives in Postgres on Cloud SQL. We never squeeze the operation into the CRM, and the CRM is never a dependency for an agent to keep working.

## Why standalone and not inside the CRM

HubSpot stays our CRM and the source of truth for contacts, leads, deals, tickets and owners. The CMA is deliberately built **outside** HubSpot, on its own foundation. We looked at building it inside HubSpot and decided against it for these reasons:

1. **This is not CRM work.** A time clock that starts on login, work statuses, rosters with skill coverage, automatic logout on the scheduled end time, realtime messages to agents and gamification are workforce and call center functions. HubSpot is not designed for them. Building them inside HubSpot means forcing them into custom objects and workarounds that stay fragile and limited.
2. **Live, all-day use needs a fast database.** Every agent keeps the CMA open all day, and managers watch live views. Running that directly on HubSpot hits API rate limits and slows down. Postgres is built for many concurrent reads and writes and handles hundreds of thousands of rows without effort.
3. **We combine more than HubSpot.** Aircall calls and recordings, the shared Gmail mailbox and later Shopify, the customer portal and NetSuite all feed productivity and steering. A neutral data layer brings these together; HubSpot would only see its own part.
4. **We own the data and the logic.** Time registration affects pay and labour rules and needs a complete, reliable history under our control. Our own Postgres plus BigQuery gives us that, plus open APIs for other tools to build on.
5. **Reusable across business lines.** Built once and configured per tenant, the same CMA runs for Subscriptions, for Invest and later for Pulse4all US, without rebuilding it for each.
6. **Cost and flexibility.** The HubSpot features needed to stretch this far (custom objects at scale, advanced developer features) sit in the most expensive tiers, and we would still be limited by what HubSpot allows. A custom build costs effort up front but removes that ceiling.
7. **A directive work queue needs its own engine.** Agent productivity dropped when Pulse4all moved from Steam Connect to HubSpot with the Aircall plugin: agents choose their next task from lists instead of being served it. HubSpot's task queues are owner-based, have no leasing and cannot share a pool between agents safely. The CMA's queue engine serves the next task, leases it, retries it, closes it at the ceiling and never forgets it; HubSpot receives the outcome (decided 6 October 2026).
8. **Several CRM instances, one operation.** Pulse4all Subs and Pulse4all Invest each run their own HubSpot portal and their own Aircall account, and Pulse4all US will most likely do the same. No portal can see another portal. The one place where the whole operation comes together, people, hours, work and outcomes across business lines, has to sit outside all of them. That is the CMA: one application, one database for Pulse4all, one adapter instance per tenant, aggregation on top (decided 6 October 2026).

HubSpot remains fully part of the flow: contacts, deals and tickets live there, outcomes and field changes are written back there, and a HubSpot app card can serve as a shortcut to the CMA. Agents either do their customer work in HubSpot and Aircall, or, once the agent desk exists, in the CMA with the record read from HubSpot. If a business line ever moves to another CRM, the same holds: that is a new adapter, not a new application.

## Business lines, databases and tenants

Pulse4all's business lines are tenants in one CMA database per environment (dev and prod).

| Level | What it is | Separation | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Database | the CMA's data for Pulse4all, one per environment | its own Cloud SQL instance, logins, secrets and readers | `cma` on `cma-prod-pg` |
| Tenant | one business line | `tenant_id` and row-level security inside the database | Pulse4all Subscriptions, Pulse4all Invest, later Pulse4all US |

- Pulse4all Subscriptions and Pulse4all Invest are two tenants in **one** database; Pulse4all US is the expected third. Each tenant has its **own CRM and telephony instance** (its own HubSpot portal and Aircall account), connected through its own adapter instance with its own credentials, external ids and property and pipeline mappings. A person who works for two business lines is two users with two sets of external ids.
- **Aggregation across tenants** happens in `cma_read` and BigQuery (readers see all business lines by default) and, for management views in the Workspace, through reporting views across business lines; the operational tables and the agent desk stay strictly per tenant. Matching the same customer across portals (a Subs customer who is also an investor) is a purpose-limitation question for Yordi before it is built.
- **Regions.** The EU-only policy on the folder means a US tenant's data would live in europe-west4 as well; whether Pulse4all US needs a US region (and therefore its own database and project) is decided when US is set up.
- If a business line ever gets its own database (a US region, for example), the same migrations run there unchanged; until then it is one database per environment and a checklist.

**Layers**

- **Sources and targets.** Per tenant a CRM and a telephony tool, plus every other channel and data source that feeds the operation and its reporting. For Pulse4all: HubSpot (CRM: contacts, leads, deals, tickets, owners) and Aircall (calls, recordings, phone numbers) are the starting systems; the shared Gmail mailbox, SMS, chat and the **Customer App** (self-service: orders, address and contract changes, service requests, in-app messages) are channels, each a row in `channel`; Shopify and Juo (orders, subscriptions), NetSuite (invoices, payments) and the **Asset Management Database** (devices, installations, service status, battery and pad expiry, replacements; the tenant's asset register, for Pulse4all an Airtable base with NetSuite ids) are data sources that feed cohort attributes and generate work for agents. The CRM is also a target: outcomes, field changes and owner assignments are written back to it. Sources are connected one by one along the roadmap; the model is built for all of them from the start.
- **Adapters and the canonical model.** Postgres stores canonical entities (lead, deal, call, ticket, owner, contact reference) with a fixed shape plus `source_system`, `source_id`, `synced_at` and the original payload in `raw`. An adapter maps one source system into that shape and executes the actions the CMA issues from its outbox. An adapter runs as one *instance per tenant*, holding that tenant's credentials, portal or account id, webhook secrets and field, pipeline and owner mappings: Pulse4all's three HubSpot portals are three instances of the one HubSpot adapter, its three Aircall accounts three instances of the Aircall adapter. A second CRM is a second adapter type with the same interface. The CRM adapter interface is small and fixed: resolve a phone number to a record, read a record, write a field diff, write an engagement with a result code, assign an owner, receive new tasks. The telephony adapter: embed the softphone, start a call, receive call events and recordings, read and set the agent's availability. Every source also maps into one more canonical entity, the **activity event** (source system, source id, channel, actor resolved through `app_user_external_id`, `occurred_at`, outcome reference, customer and cohort references): calls, dispositions, emails sent, chats and SMS handled, tickets closed, app interactions and lead assignments are all rows of this shape. It is the universal join between what happened in a channel and who was on the clock when it happened (see Features 5). HubSpot and Aircall are the first implementations; today the adapters are Make scenarios, n8n on Cloud Run EU is the second automation runtime; a second CRM is a second mapping, not a schema change. The ingest API contract is written against the canonical entities, never against a vendor payload.

Reporting across business lines (Kira over Subs and Invest, finance over all three) is a customer-level reader role on `cma_read` and BigQuery, not a tenant role; how the Workspace shows a cross-tenant Report surface to such a person is an open decision (tenant picker with an "all business lines" view on reporting views only). Roles are rows per tenant, seeded from one default ladder and adjustable; the permissions they bundle come from a catalog defined by the application code. A grant can be scoped (a supervisor for one team, a manager for one market). Users are not only Pulse4all's own employees: each user belongs to an organisation (the company itself, the call center partner, …), so hours and results can be reported per employer. The same person in two business lines is two users. A manager may edit agents and the other non-managing roles; an admin may edit anyone (`users.manage_agents` versus `users.manage_all`, enforced in the database since migration 0004). `app_user` gains a worker kind (person, AI agent) when the first AI agent works a queue; everything that attributes work and time to a user applies to both. A pulse4all.com account that reaches "no access yet" is added by a manager or admin on People (the demo's approval queue stays an open idea; access requests are not stored), or invited by email (since addition 0008l, 15 October 2026): an invitation carries one of the tenant's assignable roles (role rows, never a name in code; the system role `scheduler` never), within the inviter's ceiling, the same rule as assigning the role (`users.manage_agents` gives the non-managing roles, `users.manage_all` every one), checked again when the invitation is accepted. `app_user.kind` (`person`, `system`) and `app_role.is_assignable` exist since 0005a for the scheduler's system user; the worker kind for AI agents joins `kind` later.

**Role and surface (17 October 2026).** Which of the three surfaces of the Workspace a person gets, the Dashboard, the Cockpit or the Desk (What we are building), follows from their permissions, never from the role key: read permissions alone (`reports.view`, `performance.team`, `monitoring.live`) give the Dashboard, the figures and signals without handles; a write permission next to them (`roster.manage`, `workday.team`, `users.manage_agents`, `messages.send`, `leads.manage`, `tenant.configure`) turns the same screens into the Cockpit, each handle shown to the person who holds its permission and checked by the database like every write; `workday.own` with the agent's permissions (`leads.accept`, later the queue's) gives the Desk. In the default ladder analytics is a dashboard, the supervisor and the manager a cockpit, the agent a desk; a tenant moves a role from one surface to another by a grant, as with every permission here, and Preview as role below shows an admin what each one sees.

**Preview as role (16 October 2026, N16-A).** An admin, a person whose real permissions hold `users.manage_all`, can look at the Workspace as any of the tenant's assignable roles from the select in the top bar: the Welcome blocks, the rail, the pages and the JSON API follow the intersection of the admin's real permissions and the role's (`src/lib/auth/preview.ts`, applied in `getAccess()` after `findPrincipal` for a granted access), a banner says so and Exit preview ends it; the cookie `cma-preview-role` (httpOnly, secure, samesite=lax, path `/`, 12 hours: a preview must not outlive the day) is set and cleared by `POST /api/v1/me/preview` and cleared by Log out. A preview narrows, never widens: an unknown role, an ill-formed key or a non-admin's cookie is ignored, and the database runs every call with the real permissions (`cma.current_user_id()` is the real user), so nothing can be granted through a preview and nothing the database does changes; a previewing admin gets the role's screens and the app's 403 on the routes the role may not call. The data shown is always the person's own (own day, own hours): the preview changes visibility, not identity, and the audit's actor stays the real user. It is not impersonation; acting as someone else (backlog CMA-017) stays a separate, later question. Checked by `web/verify/preview.mjs` (pure: the intersection as a property over random sets, unknown role and non-admin ignored, the empty intersection allowed and still landing on Welcome, `canPreview` for the admin with or without a preview, the cookie's name and 12 hours), the layout and theme verifiers (the banner and the select for the admin, nothing for a manager or an agent) and `verify/api.mjs --preview`.

**Per-person reports (18 October 2026, night run N19-A).** A report's team figures are for `reports.view` or `performance.team`; its figures per person need `performance.team` and the report's key in the tenant setting `reports.per_person` (comma-separated keys from the fixed list `email`, `orders` and, since addition 0008y, `attempts`; empty by default, so a new tenant shows none). While a key is off, the per-person page (Email per agent, Orders per person, Attempts per agent), its route (404) and its rail item are absent, and the database refuses the read (`CMA06`). Pulse4all's value is `email` for Pulse4all Subscriptions (Yordi agreed, Decision log, 18 October 2026; seed `02`) and empty for Pulse4all Invest; Orders per person and Attempts per agent stay off until Yordi confirms them. The dev seed `03` holds `email,orders,attempts` once `92` has run (`email,orders` before it). Calls per agent (14 October 2026) and the per-person tables of Follow-up and of the callbacks kept are not under the setting: `performance.team` alone, as decided for each.

**Teams and skills (decided 6 October 2026).** Three dimensions, all tenant configuration:

| Dimension | What it is | Shape | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Teams | Membership, not proficiency. Pulse4all: Team EN, Team NL, Team DE, Team FR, Team Nordics | `team` with one or more markets and validity; `team_member` with validity; an agent can be in several teams | Queue hard filter, Live board and roster grouping, message targeting, scope of a supervisor or manager grant |
| 1 Languages | Proficiency with a four-step scale: Basic, Good, Fluent, Native | `skill` with dimension `language`; `user_skill` with level and validity; the scale is tenant configuration | Queue filter with a minimum level per task, message targeting, coverage |
| 2 Work types | Sales, Operations, Debt, and whatever a tenant adds | `skill` with dimension `work_type`; binary for Pulse4all, levels allowed by configuration | Queue hard filter, roster coverage, productivity per work type |

A third skill dimension, channel (phone, email, chat), stays in the model as a dimension a tenant may leave empty. Nordics shows why team and language are separate: one team, several markets, and per agent Swedish Native, Danish Good. A task declares team, required language with minimum level and work type; eligibility is derived at serve time, no precomputed table (the AgentUI trial measured 2.6 ms for 5,000 queued tasks).

## Authentication

| Version | Approach | Condition |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| V1 | Simple login screen where the agent enters their external user id | Build and test only, with own team and test data. Not built for Pulse4all, which starts on V2 |
| V2 | Real login through the tenant's identity provider (Google Workspace, Microsoft, HubSpot OAuth or local accounts), identity passed securely | Required before the first real agent registers hours (Roadmap step 2). Pulse4all: Google Workspace through IAP |

Identity is matched on the external id in `app_user_external_id`, never on the email address, so a renamed address or the domain of someone's CRM seat does not matter. A login provider's system name cannot end in `_owner` or `_user`: those names are reserved for the linked accounts on People, so `cma.add_person` refuses them as a login system and the login lookup never matches them (addition 0008e, Decision log 13 October 2026). An invitation by email (addition 0008l, 15 October 2026) does not change this: it binds the sign-in id at acceptance, and only when the address the sign-in provider verified (IAP's token) equals the invited one; from then on the person is matched on that id like everyone else, and an address alone never links or finds anyone (a person with the address added meanwhile makes the invitation invalid).

**Pulse4all: Google Workspace through IAP** (decided 4 October 2026). Every Pulse4all user, Newco agents included, has a full pulse4all.com Google Workspace account. Two layers, one list:

- **Gate (IAP)** proves who the visitor is. It admits `group:cma@pulse4all.com` and `domain:pulse4all.com` since 12 October 2026 (the agent go-live); `app_user` decides who may work
- **CMA (`app_user`)** decides who may work, with which role, employer and tenant. A pulse4all.com account without an active `app_user` row gets a "no access yet" page from the app and appears in Team as an access request. Nobody maintains a per-agent Google group
- The app verifies IAP's signed token (`x-goog-iap-jwt-assertion`) on every request and does not trust the plain identity headers. *Implemented 5 October 2026 in `web/src/proxy.ts` and `web/src/lib/auth/iap.ts`: ES256 against Google's IAP key set, issuer, audience, expiry and a future-issue check; the verified identity travels to the app in headers only the proxy can set, incoming copies are stripped; the token verifier (`web/verify/iap-token.mjs`) covers valid, missing, wrong audience, wrong issuer, expired, future issue, wrong signer and spoofed headers*
- The match key is the Google account's stable numeric id (IAP's `sub` claim is `accounts.google.com:<id>`; `iap.ts` strips the prefix and refuses any `sub` that is not that prefix plus digits), stored as system `google` in `app_user_external_id`; the email address is display only. The identity carries its provider (`google` behind IAP, `mock` in dev) in a third header only the proxy sets, `x-cma-identity-provider`, and the login looks up provider plus subject. In the app this is `findPrincipal(identity)` on the data interface (see API). In dev, where the web app runs behind Cloud Run IAM instead of IAP, the identity is a mock identity (provider `mock`) and the dev seed stores its subjects (`agent-one`, `agent-two`, `supervisor`, `manager`, `analyst`, `admin`) under system `mock`, so a test subject can never match a real Google row. The subject is chosen per request: header `x-cma-mock-subject` (verifiers), or `?as=<subject>` once in the browser, which sets a cookie; an invalid subject is a 400, never a fallback. In iap mode the header, cookie and `?as=` are ignored. `agent-one` exists in two dev tenants on purpose and gets "no access yet" until a tenant picker exists
- People with a Google login are added per tenant with the add-a-person script in the Studio editor (see Code); each person reads their own id with `curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $(gcloud auth print-access-token)" https://www.googleapis.com/oauth2/v3/userinfo` (the `sub` field), or a Workspace admin reads it with the Admin SDK Directory API `users.get` (the `id` field; the key in the Admin console's address is not this id). The ids are pasted into the Studio editor when running, never committed. Nobody signs in with a colleague's account to test their access: since 7 October 2026 a visit opens no workday, but the day, status and permissions on screen are still that person's
- **Invitations (addition 0008l, 15 October 2026).** Besides Add a person with the account id, a manager or admin invites by work email; the link (`/invite/<token>`) sits behind IAP like every page, and IAP admits only accounts it admits today (`cma@pulse4all.com`, `domain:pulse4all.com`), so an invitation reaches a pulse4all.com account only. Pulse4all therefore limits invitations to pulse4all.com (`auth.invitation_email_domains`, set for both tenants in seed `02`, 15 October 2026): an invitation to another domain is refused when it is created (400), instead of giving a link nobody can accept. Accepting binds the account's id, as above
- **Log out and the signed-out page (15 October 2026; in prod since that day).** Log out ends the day and has IAP clear its cookie; IAP then redirects to the address without the parameter. With `_LOGOUT_TARGET=legacy` that is `/logout/done`, behind IAP, so a browser with one Google account is signed straight back in. With `_LOGOUT_TARGET=signed-out`, set on `cma-web-main` since 15 October 2026 after `docs/signed-out/deploy.sh` and its test plan, it is `/signed-out` (IAP may add `?__gcp_iap_oauth_prompt=1` when it clears its cookie), a static page the load balancer serves from a public bucket outside IAP: no identity, no data, no cookie; the bucket is readable by object name only (`allUsers` `roles/storage.legacyObjectReader`, no listing), and three route rules for the Workspace's host decide what bypasses IAP: `/signed-out` and files under `/signed-out/` reach the bucket, `/signed-out/` redirects to `/signed-out`, `/signed-out` with `gcp-iap-mode=CLEAR_LOGIN_COOKIE` goes to `cma-web-backend`, and every other path, `/signed-outx` included, stays behind IAP; Log in (one account: silent; several: Google's chooser), Add another account (Google's add-account form, plain `AddSession`, in a new tab and without a return address, since Google refuses one outside Google: the person adds the account there, closes that tab and chooses it with Log in; a caption under the link says so) and, for a shared computer, a link to log out of Google. The app never signs anyone out of Google; IAP stays the gate for everything else
- Suspending someone's Google account ends their access to the CMA at once; two-factor follows the Google Workspace policy
- This is Pulse4all's configuration. The identity provider is tenant configuration: another provider changes only the gate; the user list and external-id matching stay the same. Because a person may exist in two tenants, login includes a tenant lookup across tenants (`cma.find_tenants_for_identity`, a `SECURITY DEFINER` function owned by `cma_owner` that returns only tenant ids, migration 0002) and a tenant picker when more than one matches.

**CRM access for the desk.** Reading records live and writing diffs and engagements needs a HubSpot project app (static or OAuth) per tenant with read and write scopes on contacts, companies, deals, tickets and engagements; the token lives in Secret Manager and is used only by the adapter and the outbox worker, never by the browser.

A HubSpot app card (UI extension) can be added as a convenience shortcut. App cards appear on CRM records (contact, deal, ticket), not as a fixed main-menu item, and availability depends on the HubSpot subscription. The CMA itself is the home base, not a sub-page of HubSpot.
