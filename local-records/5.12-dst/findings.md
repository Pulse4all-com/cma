# 5.12 DST readiness: 25 October 2026 (autumn clock change, Europe/Amsterdam)

**Summary.** Examined every place the CMA turns instants into days, hours and local times (db/05, 09, 13, 17, 19, 23, 26, 32, 40, 46, 50, 60, 62, 72, 74; web/src/lib/time.ts, period.ts, roster.ts, live.ts, dashboard.ts, corrections.ts, WorkdayPanel.tsx, jobs/close-forgotten-workdays.mjs; ingest/src/jobs/reconcile.mjs, core/sync.mjs; docs/scheduler/deploy.sh, docs/ingest/deploy.sh) by static review plus a rolled-back proof on the local PostgreSQL 16 with two throwaway tenants and 16 synthetic people (`01_dst_proof.sql`, output `01_dst_proof.out` in this directory), a Node run of the web helpers in UTC and in Europe/Amsterdam (`02_web_dst.mjs`, `02_web_dst.out`), the pure web verifiers and the database verify scripts 33, 61, 63, 75 (all PASS locally). About 2.5 hours.
**Most important:** no High finding. Every hours figure is computed on instants (`timestamptz`); the business day of 25 October 2026 is 25 hours long and bounded correctly; the window, the forgotten-day close, the exports, the reports and the web clock behave the same on both sides of the change. Two Low findings: the nightly ingest reconcile is scheduled at 02:30 in a zone that repeats 02:30 that night (harmless if it runs twice, a 23-hour hole in the safety net if Cloud Scheduler skips it), and the legacy import silently reads an ambiguous autumn local time as standard time while it refuses the spring gap (relevant only to a future historical import, not to the loaded `steam-2026` batch). Two Info observations.
**Spring 2026 import question:** the loaded batch is not affected (the load refuses any session in the skipped hour, so none was in it; a session spanning it is computed on instants; the 07:00 to 22:00 window clips the night anyway).

The dates: the write path (`cma.set_status`, `cma.end_workday`, `cma.correct_workday`), the close (`now()`-based) and `cma.stage_import_batch` (period before today) refuse the future, so those proofs use the previous autumn change, 2025-10-26 03:00 CEST to 02:00 CET, which the output's section A shows follows the same zone rule as 2026-10-25. The pure bound functions, the views (rows the owner inserted), the roster and the web helpers are proved on 2026-10-25 itself.

### DST-01: The nightly ingest reconcile is scheduled at 02:30 Europe/Amsterdam, a local time that occurs twice on 25 October 2026 and not at all on 29 March
- Severity: Low   Confidence: Medium   Effort: S
- Component: `docs/ingest/deploy.sh` (lines 81, 243 to 259: `RECONCILE_EVERY="30 2 * * *"`, `schedule()` with `--time-zone="Europe/Amsterdam"`), `ingest/src/jobs/reconcile.mjs`, `ingest/src/core/sync.mjs`
- Evidence: observed in the code: `reconcile.mjs:22` `DEFAULT_LOOKBACK_MINUTES = 26 * 60`; each run lists everything modified in `[now - 26 h, now]` per connection and object type and hands it to `recordAndProcess`, which records synthetic events keyed `sync:<object type>:<id>:<version>` (`sync.mjs:27`) and processes only rows `isNew` (`sync.mjs:53`), so an object already read with the same version is counted `skipped`; the upsert is guarded by the source's updated time (stale guard, DESIGN 2). The Aircall adapter splits the window into fixed 24-hour blocks of milliseconds (`aircall/reconcile.mjs:39-43`), HubSpot searches by `hs_lastmodifieddate` between `sinceMs` and `untilMs`, Shopify by `updated_at` (ISO instants): none of the three uses a local date. What Cloud Scheduler does with a `30 2 * * *` schedule in a zone on the repeated hour (once, twice or neither) is not decidable from this repository: a Browser/Gemini check for Martin. Estimated from the code: **run twice** (00:30Z and 01:30Z): the second run lists the same objects, every key is known, `skipped` counts them, nothing is written twice. **Run once** (either instant): consecutive runs 25 h apart against a 26 h look-back, still covered with 1 h of overlap instead of 2 h. **Not run at all**: the run of 24 Oct 00:30Z covered up to 24 Oct 00:30Z, the next run is 26 Oct 01:30Z (02:30 CET) covering from 24 Oct 23:30Z, so modifications between 24 Oct 00:30Z and 23:30Z (23 hours) are never reconciled. Webhooks stay the primary path and the sweep (every minute) retries recorded events, so the hole is in the safety net only. The every-minute schedules (sweep, poll-forms, poll-email) fire each minute whatever the hour is called and are unaffected; the hourly `cma-close-forgotten-workdays-hourly` runs in `Etc/UTC` (`docs/scheduler/deploy.sh:52-62`) and is unaffected (check 9).
- Impact (in this system's context): once a year, if the scheduler skips the repeated hour, a day of HubSpot, Aircall and Shopify changes that lost their webhook is not caught until someone runs the reconcile by hand; no hours, pay or billing figure depends on it. If it runs twice: nothing.
- Recommendation: move the nightly run off the 02:00 to 03:00 hour so neither change touches it, keeping the local-time decision of 10 October 2026 (reconcile nightly): a schedule-only change with the existing flag, no code. **Terminal** (Cloud Shell; 03:30 is as quiet as 02:30): `bash docs/ingest/deploy.sh prod --jobs --reconcile-every "30 3 * * *"` (expected output ends with `schedule ingest-reconcile: 30 3 * * * (Europe/Amsterdam)`); the same for dev. Then **Terminal**: `gcloud scheduler jobs describe ingest-reconcile --project=p4a-cma-prod --location=europe-west4 --format='value(schedule,timeZone)'`, expected `30 3 * * *	Europe/Amsterdam`. And in the repository, so a later `--jobs` deploy without the flag does not put it back (unified diff, proposed, not applied):
  ```
  --- a/docs/ingest/deploy.sh
  +++ b/docs/ingest/deploy.sh
  @@
  -#   --reconcile-every "<cron>"       with --jobs: the reconcile schedule (default "30 2 * * *", Europe/Amsterdam)
  +#   --reconcile-every "<cron>"       with --jobs: the reconcile schedule (default "30 3 * * *", Europe/Amsterdam:
  +#                     03:30 lies outside the 02:00 to 03:00 hour that the autumn change repeats and the spring change skips)
  @@
  -RECONCILE_EVERY="30 2 * * *"
  +RECONCILE_EVERY="30 3 * * *"
  @@
  -if [ "$JOBS" = 0 ] && { [ "$RECONCILE_EVERY" != "30 2 * * *" ] || [ -n "$RECONCILE_LOOKBACK" ]; }; then
  +if [ "$JOBS" = 0 ] && { [ "$RECONCILE_EVERY" != "30 3 * * *" ] || [ -n "$RECONCILE_LOOKBACK" ]; }; then
  ```
  plus the mention of 02:30 in the header comment of `ingest/src/jobs/reconcile.mjs:2` and the README (Decision log 10 October 2026, "reconcile (02:30)", and the ingest paragraph) in the same PR. Alternative kept out: scheduling in `Etc/UTC` would move the local run time by an hour every half year.
- README check: new. Decision log 10 Oct 2026 (decisions index line 1816: "reconcile (02:30)") fixed the time, not the zone's DST behaviour; nothing in Open decisions.
- Disproof attempt: looked for a local-date assumption in the three adapters' reconcile windows (none: milliseconds throughout) and for a second dedupe path that would break on a double run (none needed: the event key dedupes before processing; `cma.ingest_record_events` returns `is_new`). Tried to show the hole is covered by something else: the sweep only retries events already recorded, so a webhook that never arrived is not in it. The finding stands only in the "not run at all" case, hence Low and Confidence Medium.

### DST-02: The legacy import reads an ambiguous autumn local time as standard time without a count or a refusal, while it refuses the spring gap
- Severity: Low   Confidence: High   Effort: M
- Component: `db/50_legacy_time_import.sql` lines 424 to 431 (the spring-gap refusal), 473 to 477 (`s.login_local at time zone b.timezone`); `docs/import/README.md` line 211
- Evidence: observed on the local database (`01_dst_proof.out`, check 5a): on a throwaway tenant without a window, batch `dst-autumn-2025` with three synthetic sessions on 2025-10-26 loaded with no refusal and these days: `A1 01:30-02:30 = 7200 s` (the logout `02:30` became `01:30Z`, CET), `A2 02:30-03:30 = 3600 s`, `A3 01:00-04:00 = 14400 s` (four real hours where the wall clock shows three: correct on instants). Observed on the same run (5b): a session with a login at `2026-03-29 02:30` is refused as a whole, `CMA04 1 session(s) fall in the spring clock change gap of Europe/Amsterdam`, and one spanning the gap (`01:30-03:30`) loads as 3600 s. Expected from the code: the gap check `(local at time zone z) at time zone z <> local` cannot see an ambiguous time (it round-trips), so a session that Steam logged out at 02:30 CEST would be stored an hour later and count one hour too long; the autumn hour of 2025 is inside Steam's history before 2 January 2026, which is not loaded. `docs/import/README.md:211` warns about the spring gap only; the README (0008d, "Imported days are kept in the batch's zone") states the behaviour.
- Impact (in this system's context): none for the loaded `steam-2026` batch (2 January to 9 October 2026: no autumn change in the period; the Pulse4all window 07:00 to 22:00 clips the night to nothing anyway, so a night session is counted as `sessions_outside_window`, never as hours). It becomes a time-figure error of one hour per affected session only for a future import that covers an autumn change on a tenant whose window includes 02:00 to 03:00 (the universal default 00:00 to 24:00).
- Recommendation: (1) one line in `docs/import/README.md` next to line 211: "a local time between 02:00 and 02:59 on the autumn change date is read as standard time (the later instant); check the source's export for that hour before staging". (2) A later migration pair (0008q or the next free pair, not applied here) that counts the ambiguous sessions in the batch's `counts` (`sessions_ambiguous_local`) and refuses the load when any of them lies inside the window, mirroring the spring check. Sketch for the verify to settle (an ambiguous local time is one whose instant lies more than one hour after the instant of `local - 1 hour`):
  ```sql
  -- in cma.load_import_batch, after the spring-gap check
  select count(*), min(s.external_session_id) into v_n, v_txt
  from cma.import_session s
  where s.tenant_id = v_tenant and s.batch_id = b.id
    and ((s.login_local at time zone b.timezone) - ((s.login_local - interval '1 hour') at time zone b.timezone) > interval '1 hour'
         or (s.logout_local is not null
             and (s.logout_local at time zone b.timezone) - ((s.logout_local - interval '1 hour') at time zone b.timezone) > interval '1 hour'));
  ```
  Not a change I can stand behind without its verify script, hence proposed.
- README check: settled (0008d, 12 Oct 2026: "an ambiguous local time would take PostgreSQL's reading (the later, standard-time instant)"); the new information is only that the import runbook does not carry the note and that a historical (2025) import would meet it.
- Disproof attempt: checked whether the staging trigger or `stage_import_batch` catches it (no: `check_import_row` checks the batch state and the map only); checked whether the window clips it for Pulse4all (yes, which is why the loaded data is clean and the severity is Low).
